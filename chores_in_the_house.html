Lesson objectives

  • use common collocations for household chores;
  • describe personal routines and preferences;
  • express and support opinions;
  • make predictions about future homes.

Vocabulary

1

Match the chores

A. Click an image and then the matching sentence. B. Make the sentences true about you. C. Discuss your chore preferences using the prompts.

Speaking prompts
I like … I hate … … is really boring. … takes too much time. I have to … every day. I’d like to pay someone to … for me.

Video 1 · Children and chores

Watch for detail, then discuss responsibility, pocket money and fairness.

2

Watch the video

Watch the video to 01:11 and focus on the chores the boy does.

Should children get money for doing chores?
Is $30 a week a fair amount?
Which chores did you do as a child?
Which chores should be unpaid family responsibilities?
At what age should children start helping at home?
Does payment teach responsibility or create entitlement?
3

What does the boy do?

Tick the chores, then check your answers.

Answer: unload the dishwasher, walk the dog and make the bed.
4

Sort the responsibilities

Choose whether each chore should be unpaid, paid, or depends on the situation. Explain your choices.

Video 2 · Robots at home

Use the video to make predictions, evaluate technology and design an ideal household robot.

5

Watch and notice

Write down at least five things the robot can do.

6

Choose and justify

Choose a word to give your opinion and add some details.

I like to have the robot in the video in my house.
Robots do household chores better than people.
In 2050, robots do all household chores.
A home robot be allowed to enter every room.
People become lazier if robots did all the chores.
A robot collect information about people’s habits.

Choose one option in each sentence, then explain and support your opinion orally.
