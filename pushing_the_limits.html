4. It’s reading time!

Read the facts about extreme sports and discuss the questions.

Mount Everest

More than 340 people have died while climbing or descending (= going down) Mount Everest. Some of them died while waiting in a long queue (= line of people) before they could reach the top.
  • Why do you think people continue to climb Everest, even though it’s so crowded (= full of people) and dangerous?
  • Imagine your friend wanted to climb Everest. Would you encourage (= support / say yes) or discourage them? Why?

Free soloing

Alex Honnold was the first person to free solo El Capitan – a nearly 900-metre vertical rock face (= very steep rock wall) – without ropes. It has been described as one of the greatest athletic achievement (= something impressive done) s.
  • How do you think journalists should report (= write or speak about) on extreme achievement (= something impressive done) s like this one? How shouldn’t they report (= write or speak about) on them?
  • Are people born with a desire (= strong wish) to take deadly risks or is this desire (= strong wish) something they learn from others? Why do you think so?

Long-distance swimming

Diana Nyad swam from Cuba to Florida (about 177 km) without a shark cage (= metal cage for safety) at the age of 64 after several failed attempts (= tries) .
  • Why do you think Diana Nyad was so determined (= not giving up) to succeed? Do you think you could be as determined (= not giving up) ?
  • What do you think Diana did to prepare for this achievement (= something impressive done) ?

Ironman Triathlon

An Ironman Triathlon is a 226-km race (= sport competition) that combines a 3.8-km swim, a 180-km bike ride and a full marathon in a single day (= one day only) . Athletes often spend months or years preparing for the race (= sport competition) .
  • What do you think is harder: preparing for an Ironman Triathlon or completing (= finishing) it?
  • Would you ever consider (= think about doing) taking part in a race (= sport competition) like this? Why/Why not?
