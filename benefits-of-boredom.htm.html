Article · Part 1

It’s more than OK for kids to be bored − it’s good for them

by Margaret Murray, University of Michigan-Dearborn, in The Conversation (article adapted)

I’m a professor who studies communication and culture. I am currently writing a book about modern parenting, and I’ve noticed that many parents try to help their kids avoid boredom.

There are different reasons parents do that. If they are busy with work and other daily responsibilities, they often let their child watch a TV show or play a game. They don’t want to hear the child complain about how bored they are.

Parents may also feel that their child should do well in school. Because of that, children nowadays spend less time playing freely outside and more time in organized activities than they did in the past.

Many parents had to put their children in front of screens during the pandemic to keep them busy during work hours. More recently, some parents have said that people expect them to use screens to keep children quiet in public.

Benefits of boredom

Although boredom feels bad in the moment, it real benefits for personal growth. It is a sign that a change is needed. Psychologists have found that boredom can lead us to discover new goals and to try new activities.

Additionally, we need boredom because it gives us time to the big questions in life. Boredom also makes us want to learn things and it makes us creative.

That is why parents shouldn’t boredom as a problem that they must solve for their children.

Research shows that young people who used screens to calm down found it difficult to their emotions as they grew older.
