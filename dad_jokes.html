Lesson objectives

By the end of the lesson, you can:

1identify homophones and homographs in context;
2explain how wordplay creates humour;
3pronounce common homographs accurately;
4deliver and discuss jokes confidently.

Tip: a dad joke is often deliberately simple, predictable or “cheesy”. The language is the real challenge.

Start with humour, notice the double meanings and discover the difference between homophones and homographs.

6

Tell the jokes

Work in pairs. Open only your own list. Read each joke to your partner. If your partner does not understand it, use the short explanation under the joke.

Student A

Open your joke set

How can you tell a vampire has a cold?
He starts coffin.

Why it is funny: coffin sounds like coughing. Vampires sleep in coffins, and people with a cold cough.

Why couldn’t the bad sailor learn the alphabet?
Because he always got lost at ‘C’.

Why it is funny: the letter C sounds like sea. A bad sailor gets lost at sea.

What did one wall say to the other wall?
I’ll meet you at the corner!

Why it is funny: two walls physically meet at a corner, so the phrase is both literal and conversational.

I’m so good at sleeping that I do it with my eyes closed.

Why it is funny: everyone normally sleeps with their eyes closed, so the “special skill” is not special at all.

What did one hat say to the other hat?
You stay here, I’ll go on ahead!

Why it is funny: ahead sounds like a head. A hat goes on a head.

Student B

Open your joke set

Why was six afraid of seven?
Because seven ate nine.

Why it is funny: eight and ate sound the same, so “seven eight nine” becomes “seven ate nine”.

Do you want to hear a joke about construction?
I’m still working on it.

Why it is funny: working on it means both creating the joke and physically working on a construction project.

I was kidnapped by a gang of mimes once. They threatened to do unspeakable things to me.

Why it is funny: mimes do not speak, so the things they do are literally “unspeakable”.

I love telling Dad jokes. Sometimes, he even laughs.

Why it is funny: Dad jokes usually means a type of joke, but here it is understood as jokes told to the speaker’s actual dad.

Can February March?
No, but April May!

Why it is funny: March and May are months, but they can also be verbs.
