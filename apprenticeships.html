Useful vocabulary

  • career path — work direction
  • on-the-job training — learning at work
  • aptitude test — ability test
  • train to be — prepare for profession
  • practical experience — real work experience
  • mentor — experienced adviser
  • trade — skilled practical job
  • certified — officially qualified

2. Video: Apprenticeships

Watch the video and then personalise the ideas by connecting them to a job you know well.

Discuss the Questions
  • Do you think the video can encourage people to do apprenticeships? Why/Why not?
  • Are young people in your country interested in trade jobs? Why/Why not?
  • How popular are apprenticeships in your country? Have they become more popular or less popular in recent years?
  • Why would someone choose to do an apprenticeship later in life?
  • Do older adults do apprenticeships in your country? What challenges do they face?
  • If you were going to do an apprenticeship, what profession would you choose?

Think about a job

Choose your own job, a previous job, or any profession you know well. Decide which statements could describe the way people enter or learn this profession. Explain your answers.

  • You usually have one career path and it is not easy to change career.
  • You get on-the-job training from experts, but you also study using course materials.
  • You have to take an aptitude test to show that you are suitable for the profession.
  • You train to be a particular professional without learning everything about the whole industry.
  • The main purpose is to gain practical experience.
  • You have a mentor who supports you and answers your questions.
  • After completing the training, you become a certified professional and can start working in the field.
  • The training allows you to learn a trade.
