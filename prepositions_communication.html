Prepositions of Communication, Opinions, Interaction

AGREE ON • OBJECT TO • APOLOGISE FOR • WARN ABOUT • REMIND OF

1. Agreeing, Disagreeing & Public Opinions
News / video
Elon Musk says “AI will make work optional” 🤯
Discussion questions
1. What part of the statement do you agree or disagree with?
AGREE ABOUT somethingOBJECT TO something / doing something
2. Is there any idea, proposal, or decision here that you would accept or oppose?
AGREE TO somethingAPPROVE OF something
3. What solution could people agree on, and would you argue for or against it?
AGREE ON somethingARGUE FOR somethingARGUE / PROTEST AGAINST something
Language bank
We AGREE ABOUT (the same view) the main problem.
We finally AGREED ON (reach the same decision) a solution.
They AGREED TO (accept) the proposal.
I APPROVE OF (think it is good / right) the decision.
I OBJECT TO (oppose) the decision.
I would ARGUE FOR (support) stricter rules.
I would ARGUE / PROTEST AGAINST (oppose) stricter rules.
Check your knowledge
We agree the main problem.
The team finally agreed one solution.
They agreed the proposal.
I don't approve replacing people completely with AI.
Many employees object being monitored at work.
Some experts argue stricter rules.
Thousands protested the new policy.

Discussion questions

2. What would you comment on if you saw this online?
COMMENT ON somethingFOCUS ON something
3. Would you discuss this story with friends? What part would you mention first?
DISCUSS / MENTION somethingREFER TO somebody / something
Language bank
People REACTED TO / RESPONDED TO the announcement.
I would COMMENT ON (give an opinion) the price.
I would FOCUS ON the long-term effects.
I would DISCUSS / MENTION the story with friends.

She mentioned the cost several times.
The speaker referred job losses.

2. Who would people complain to, and what would they complain about?
COMPLAIN TO somebodyCOMPLAIN ABOUT somethingCRITICISE somebody FOR something / doing something

make an EXCUSE FOR something

4. Accusations, Warnings & Persuasion
News / video
Polish PM Tusk issues stark warning that Putin could be planning to bomb UK as NATO on red alert 👀🔴
Discussion questions
1. What are people being warned about?
WARN somebody ABOUT somethingWARN somebody OF somethingASSURE somebody OF something
2. Who is being accused or suspected of doing something wrong?
ACCUSE somebody OF something / doing somethingSUSPECT somebody OF something / doing something
3. What could convince or persuade people to change their opinion or behaviour? Who might this message appeal to?
CONVINCE somebody OF somethingPERSUADE somebody TO do somethingAPPEAL TO somebody
Language bank
Experts WARNED people ABOUT (tell them about a risk / problem) the situation.
Experts WARNED people OF (alert them to a specific danger) a possible attack.
I would ASSURE people OF (tell them confidently) their safety.
The company was ACCUSED OF (say someone did something wrong) misleading customers.
They SUSPECTED him OF (think someone may have done it) hiding information.
The evidence CONVINCED me OF (make someone believe) the problem.
What would PERSUADE you TO (make someone decide to act) change your mind?
The campaign APPEALED TO (attract / interest) young voters.
SHOW OFF (try to impress people)
Check your knowledge
Officials warned people possible delays.
The report warned the public the danger.
The company assured customers the safety of their data.
He was accused hiding information.
Police suspected him leaking the documents.
The evidence convinced me the need for change.
What could persuade you change your mind?
The message appealed younger voters.

2. What would you ask this person?
ASK somebody a question
3. How would you reply or explain your opinion to them?
REPLY / WRITE TO somebodyEXPLAIN / DESCRIBE something TO somebodyPHONE / CALL / EMAIL / TEXT somebody
Language bank
She TALKED / SPOKE TO (directed her words to) the manager.
She TALKED / SPOKE WITH (had a conversation with) a colleague.
They TALKED / SPOKE ABOUT (topic) the new policy.
He SPOKE ON (gave a formal talk / presentation on) AI at the conference.

I would EXPLAIN the problem TO them.
I would CALL / EMAIL / TEXT them directly.
Check your knowledge

They talked the new policy.
He spoke AI regulation at the conference.
Please reply my email.
She wrote the company yesterday.
Can you explain the problem me?

6. Social Interaction, Trust & Recognition
News / video
Mr. Bean Just Walks Off Stage After Receiving An Award
Discussion questions
1. Who deserves to be congratulated or complimented, and why?
CONGRATULATE somebody ON somethingCONGRATULATE somebody FOR doing somethingCOMPLIMENT somebody ON something
2. Who would you trust with information in this situation?
TRUST somebody WITH somethingREMIND somebody ABOUT something
3. Does this story remind you of anyone or anything similar?
REMIND somebody OF somebody / something
Language bank
I would CONGRATULATE her ON (achievement / event) the promotion.
I would CONGRATULATE her FOR (action / effort) handling it so well.
I would COMPLIMENT him ON his response.
I wouldn't TRUST everyone WITH that information.
I'd REMIND them ABOUT (help them not forget) the deadline.
She REMINDS me OF (makes me think of) my sister.
Check your knowledge
Everyone congratulated her the award.
I congratulated him handling the situation well.
She complimented him his clear answer.
Would you trust this company your personal data?
Please remind me the meeting tomorrow.
That actor reminds me my old teacher.
Teacher use: Let students answer naturally first. Then ask them to answer again using 1–2 expressions from the Language Bank. For stronger groups, hide the Language Bank during the final round and ask students to reuse the patterns from memory.
