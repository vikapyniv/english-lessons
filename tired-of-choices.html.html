‘Decision fatigue’ could be hurting your health

by Emma Beckett, Australian Catholic University, in The Conversation (article adapted)

Part 1 · What is decision fatigue?

You’re standing in a supermarket aisle, thinking whether to buy a microwave meal or fresh carrots.

We all know making healthy eating choices can be difficult. That’s especially true if you are hungry, or have a hungry family to feed.

There are so many reasons for this, and many are outside our control. But one you might not know of is a concept called ‘decision fatigue’.

So what exactly is decision fatigue?

Decision fatigue describes what happens when we make many decisions over time. Every time you make a decision, you use a small amount of energy. As that energy runs low, you make worse decisions.

This means you do something without thinking, or simply choose what is easy or what you already know. This often means you have a takeaway or your favourite comfort food instead of a healthier option – cooking a meal using fresh food.

Part 2 · How might it influence my eating habits?

You may think you’re just choosing a meal. But that one decision includes other choices about what and how much you eat, as well as where, when and how you eat it.

You may make these choices automatically. But each choice means you consider a lot of factors, such as taste, cost, time, what you expect from a meal and more.

With decision fatigue, it becomes harder to make healthy choices. Instead, you may choose easier options to feel better quickly. Things around you such as adverts for fast food, snacks or sweet treats can influence your decision, too.

Not the only factor

A few other factors may have an effect on your food choices.

One is stress. One study from 2022 showed parents who experience a lot of stress and decision fatigue found it more difficult to cook meals or eat together as a family.

Another is tiredness. One 2017 study found that between mealtimes, and especially in the afternoon, people often choose simpler food and not the one that they need to think about and prepare.
