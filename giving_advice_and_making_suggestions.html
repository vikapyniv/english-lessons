Giving advice and making suggestions

1. Discuss

Choose a quote and say if you agree with it or not. Explain why.

“Good advice is always certain to be ignored, but that’s no reason not to give it.”
— Agatha Christie, author
“Sometimes, the best advice you can give is not to provide any at all.”
— Gustavo Razzetti, speaker and author
“It is always a silly thing to give advice, but to give good advice is absolutely fatal.”
— Oscar Wilde, author and poet

2. Look at the pictures

Look at each picture and read the sentence. Discuss with your partner what you think the phrasal verb means, explain how the picture supports your idea, and ask your partner what they think.

He keeps up with the latest news.

He puts off doing the laundry until tomorrow.

He turns down the job offer.

He puts up with his annoying companion.

They have run out of coffee beans.

He gives up smoking.

He works out the solution.

3. Mini-test

Choose the phrasal verb that best matches each meaning or situation.

1. Which phrasal verb means to refuse an offer or invitation?

2. “I can’t tolerate this behaviour any longer.” Which phrasal verb has the same meaning?

3. You had some coffee, but now there is none left. What happened?

4. Which phrasal verb means to stop doing something permanently?

5. You decide not to do something today and move it to a later time. Which phrasal verb describes this?

6. Which phrasal verb means to stay informed and not fall behind?

7. You think carefully and finally find the answer to a problem. Which phrasal verb describes this?

5. Give advice

Read the problem and decide what advice you would give.
  1. Choose one or two advice frames.
  2. Drag in suitable phrasal verbs and complete your advice aloud.
  3. Your partner gives their own advice.
  4. Compare your ideas and discuss the problem: Has this happened to you? What did you do? How common is it? Who might face it?

Advice frames

clear adviceYou should / shouldn’t
possible optionYou could / could avoid
gentle suggestionYou might want to / might not want to
strong warningYou’d better / You’d better not
idea to considerI would suggest / I wouldn’t suggest
stronger recommendationI would recommend / I wouldn’t recommend
personal adviceIf I were you, I would / wouldn’t
